03 / METHODOLOGY · V2.0

Evidence before
inference.

A ratio describes observed harm under specific reporting conditions. It cannot establish intent or rank an army’s respect for civilian life.

Duration and daily averages

Reporting days count both start and end dates in UTC. Deaths per day = the source’s reported deaths divided by days in that same reporting window. Lower bounds stay lower bounds. Conflict-phase age is displayed separately through the review date. We never divide a five-month toll by the whole war’s duration, extrapolate stale counts to today, or treat an average as the toll on every day. Ceasefire days remain in calendar duration. Daily normalization does not resolve different civilian definitions or weapons coverage.

What the ratio can tell you

Deaths per 1,000 fired munitions = attributable civilian deaths ÷ documented fired munitions × 1,000. Both inputs must cover the same actor, geography, period and weapon category. We withhold the result when those conditions fail. Intercepted weapons remain part of a fired count; interceptions and decoys must be documented separately.

Deaths per recorded harmful incident uses only a source’s casualty-producing incidents. Incidents may contain multiple weapons and omit attacks with no reported casualties. This metric is conditional on the reporting sample and must not be read as deaths per strike across a whole campaign.

How conflicts are selected

The target is the three conflicts with the most reported direct civilian deaths in a comparable rolling 12-month global dataset, reviewed quarterly. That ranking is not yet established here. The launch cohort uses the three highest civilian-fatality locations in AOAV’s 2025 explosive-weapons report: Gaza, Sudan and Ukraine. Deaths from other violence and indirect mortality are outside this baseline. The current cohort must not be described as the world’s definitive three deadliest wars.

Two layers, no assumed neutrality

Independent monitoring, UN verification and independent media reporting retain their distinct methods. Official claims identify the originating authority, including claims reproduced by a UN agency or news outlet. No source is treated as unbiased by default. Opposing claims are stored separately, never averaged into a supposed truth. A combined group total is never silently assigned to one member.

Weapons, civilians and attribution

Separate artillery and mortars, unguided rockets, short-range drones, one-way attack drones, ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, guided bombs, other bombs and unknown weapons where the source supports that detail. Hypersonic speed and guidance are attributes, not additive categories. Do not convert targets, sorties, launches, interceptions, stockpiles or deliveries into each other.

Retain each source’s definition of civilian. Women and children are not a substitute for all civilians; unidentified adult men are not automatically combatants. Record deaths and injuries separately. Never divide all-cause deaths by explosive weapons use, or subtract an unrelated combatant estimate from a total death toll.

Evidence updates and revisions

This is a dated evidence snapshot. Automatic updates are disabled. The last completed evidence review is displayed at the top.

Retrieve new official and open reports, preserve original links and reporting periods, identify overlaps and revisions, and publish only supported changes. If a source is inaccessible, preserve its last usable evidence and mark the gap. Report publication dates, observation periods and review dates are different. A fresh review does not mean fresh casualty data.

Limits that affect comparison

Population exposure, urban density, displacement, shelters, air defence, weapon effects, targeting practices and unequal access for monitors can all change the observed ratio. Lower values alone cannot demonstrate greater care for civilians. Harm from deprivation, displacement and damaged essential services is outside the direct-death ratio.
